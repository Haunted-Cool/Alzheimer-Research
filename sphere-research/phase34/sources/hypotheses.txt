1. Final Research Objective
The project investigates relationships between two plasma biomarkers—glial fibrillary acidic
protein (GFAP) and neurofilament light chain (NfL)—and two major measures of
Alzheimer's-related brain pathology:
● Amyloid-beta burden measured using amyloid PET.
● Aggregated tau burden measured using tau PET.
The objective is to estimate how each plasma marker is associated with continuous brain
pathology measurements when amyloid and tau PET are considered together.This is an observational association study, not a causal experiment.
The study uses synthetic multimodal records released through Stanford's Alzheimer's Disease
Research Center (ADRC) SPHERE initiative.
Scientific focus
Primary: GFAP and amyloid PET, adjusted for tau PET.
Secondary: GFAP and tau PET; NfL and amyloid PET; NfL and tau PET.
Important change from the original proposal
The original project attempted to distinguish amyloid-predominant from tau-predominant
Alzheimer's disease using opposite predicted differences in GFAP and NfL.
The revised project no longer assumes two biologically distinct Alzheimer's subtypes or
opposing biomarker relationships.
Its focus is now conditional biomarker–pathology associations.
2. Finalized Primary Research Question
The following is the exact wording retained from the completed phase:
Among Stanford ADRC SPHERE synthetic records with available plasma
biomarkers and both PET modalities, is plasma GFAP associated with continuous
amyloid-PET burden after accounting for tau-PET burden, age, and sex?
Designation: RQ1 — Primary research question
Scientific purpose: Determine whether the GFAP–amyloid association persists after
accounting for tau pathology and selected demographic covariates.
This question does not require dividing participants into positive and negative PET groups.
3. Final Primary Hypothesis — H1
Exact finalized wordingHigher amyloid-PET burden is expected to be associated with higher plasma GFAP
after adjustment for tau-PET burden, age, and sex.
Statistical formulation
● Null hypothesis: β_GFAP,A = 0
● Alternative hypothesis: β_GFAP,A ≠ 0
● Expected biological direction: Positive
● Statistical approach: Two-sided testing
The coefficient represents the conditional amyloid association in a model where plasma GFAP is
the outcome.
Biological justification
GFAP is an intermediate filament protein expressed prominently in astrocytes.
Astrocytes participate in structural support, metabolic regulation, inflammatory signaling, and
responses to neurological damage.
Amyloid accumulation can be accompanied by changes in astrocytic activity. Plasma GFAP may
reflect aspects of these responses.
Therefore, increased amyloid burden provides a biologically plausible reason to expect
increased GFAP.
However:
● GFAP is not exclusive to amyloid-associated pathology.
● Astrocytic changes can accompany other neurological processes.
● A positive association would not establish that amyloid directly caused GFAP elevation.
● The association may differ by disease stage, population, and measurement platform.
Supporting scientific evidence
Pereira et al. (2021), R01
The study examined plasma GFAP associations with amyloid and tau PET.
GFAP–amyloid relationships persisted after accounting for tau PET, whereas GFAP–tau
associations did not persist after amyloid adjustment in that study.
This is the strongest direct precedent for the primary hypothesis.
DOI: 10.1093/brain/awab223Benedet et al. (2021), R02
Plasma GFAP showed meaningful associations with amyloid-related pathology across
Alzheimer's disease populations.
The study also emphasized that plasma and cerebrospinal fluid GFAP do not perform identically.
DOI: 10.1001/jamaneurol.2021.3671
Cicognola et al. (2021), R03
Plasma GFAP was associated with CSF-defined Alzheimer's pathology and subsequent clinical
progression in a mild cognitive impairment cohort.
This supports biological relevance but is not a direct replication of the two-PET model.
DOI: 10.1186/s13195-021-00804-9
Bellaver et al. (2023), R04
Astrocyte-reactivity status influenced observed amyloid–tau relationships in preclinical disease.
The findings support the importance of astrocytic biology but do not prove that GFAP is specific
to amyloid.
DOI: 10.1038/s41591-023-02380-x
Evidence challenging or qualifying H1
Salvadó et al. (2023), R17
Plasma GFAP retained an association with postmortem tau tangle burden after adjustment for
amyloid plaques.
This challenges a universal amyloid-only interpretation.
DOI: 10.15252/emmm.202217123
Pan et al. (2025), R14
No statistically significant GFAP difference was reported between A+T− and A−T+ groups.
The original group hypothesis therefore has an existing direct precedent without the predicted
significant difference.
This does not directly refute the revised continuous H1 because the analyses estimate different
relationships.DOI: 10.1002/alz.70987
Bianco et al. (2026), R19
Plasma GFAP was associated with quantitative amyloid-related and tau-related postmortem
pathology, as well as tissue astrocytic markers.
These results support a more complex biological interpretation.
DOI: 10.1002/alz.71883
Interpretation rules
A positive adjusted coefficient would be consistent with H1 and with the Pereira precedent.
A coefficient near zero with substantial uncertainty would not prove that GFAP is unrelated to
amyloid.
A negative estimate would oppose the predicted direction and require careful evaluation.
No possible statistical outcome alone establishes amyloid-specific GFAP biology.
4. Secondary Research Question and
Hypothesis — H2
Exact finalized research question
Is plasma GFAP associated with regional tau-PET burden after accounting for
amyloid-PET burden, age, and sex?
Exact finalized hypothesis wording
The conditional GFAP–tau association is not assumed to be zero or positive; it will
be evaluated using a two-sided test.
Statistical formulation
● Null: β_GFAP,T = 0
● Alternative: β_GFAP,T ≠ 0
● Prespecified direction: None
● Designation: Secondary hypothesisThe unusual wording of H2 is preserved exactly as previously selected. Its intended meaning is
that the investigation does not assume either an absent association or a positive association
before testing.
Biological rationale
Although GFAP is often linked to amyloid-associated astrocytic responses, astrocytes may also
respond to neuronal dysfunction, inflammatory changes, and tau-associated pathology.
The biological literature does not justify assuming that GFAP has no conditional association with
tau in every population.
Relevant supporting and challenging studies
Pereira et al. (2021), R01: GFAP–tau associations did not persist after amyloid adjustment in
the investigated PET cohort.
DOI: 10.1093/brain/awab223
Bellaver et al. (2023), R04: Astrocytic reactivity was relevant to the amyloid–tau relationship.
DOI: 10.1038/s41591-023-02380-x
Salvadó et al. (2023), R17: GFAP retained an adjusted association with postmortem tangles.
DOI: 10.15252/emmm.202217123
Bianco et al. (2026), R19: GFAP related to quantitative tau-associated neuropathology
alongside amyloid and astrocytic measures.
DOI: 10.1002/alz.71883
Expected relationship
No definitive direction is predicted.
Possible outcomes include:
● A positive conditional association.
● A negligible conditional association.
● A negative conditional association.
These outcomes must be interpreted using effect sizes, confidence intervals, data quality, and
the distinction between PET and postmortem pathology.5. Secondary Research Question and
Hypothesis — H3
Exact finalized research question
Is plasma NfL associated with amyloid-PET burden after accounting for tau-PET
burden, age, and sex?
Exact finalized hypothesis wording
The conditional NfL–amyloid association may differ from zero; direction is not
prespecified.
Statistical formulation
● Null: β_NFL,A = 0
● Alternative: β_NFL,A ≠ 0
● Prespecified direction: None
● Designation: Secondary hypothesis
Biological rationale
NfL is a structural component of the neuronal cytoskeleton, particularly axons.
Elevated plasma NfL can reflect neuroaxonal injury associated with multiple neurological
processes.
Amyloid-related disease processes may contribute directly or indirectly to neuronal stress and
injury.
However, NfL elevation does not specifically identify amyloid pathology.
Supporting evidence
Benedet et al. (2020), R05
NfL exhibited relationships with amyloid PET in cognitively unimpaired participants.
The associations depended on clinical stage.
DOI: 10.1093/brain/awaa342
Mattsson et al. (2017), R06Plasma NfL was associated with Alzheimer's disease and neurodegeneration-related
measurements.
This supports NfL as an injury marker, not as an amyloid-specific marker.
DOI: 10.1001/jamaneurol.2016.6117
Evidence challenging a specific relationship
Ashton et al. (2021), R08
NfL elevations occurred in multiple neurological disorders, demonstrating limited disease
specificity.
DOI: 10.1038/s41467-021-23620-z
Dewenter et al. (2026), R20
NfL showed strong associations with small-vessel-disease burden.
This demonstrates an important competing explanation for elevated concentrations.
DOI: 10.1002/alz.71530
Expected relationship
A measurable association is biologically plausible, but neither its presence nor its direction is
assumed.
Clinical stage, vascular injury, and other neurological conditions may influence the estimate.
6. Secondary Research Question and
Hypothesis — H4
Exact finalized research question
Is plasma NfL associated with regional tau-PET burden after accounting for
amyloid-PET burden, age, and sex?
Exact finalized hypothesis wordingThe conditional NfL–tau association may differ from zero; direction is not
prespecified.
Statistical formulation
● Null: β_NFL,T = 0
● Alternative: β_NFL,T ≠ 0
● Prespecified direction: None
● Designation: Secondary hypothesis
Biological rationale
Tau pathology is associated with neuronal dysfunction and neurodegenerative processes.
Because NfL reflects neuroaxonal injury, tau-associated neurodegeneration could accompany
increased plasma NfL.
Nevertheless, tau PET measures aggregated tau burden rather than axonal injury directly.
The relationship may be nonlinear or depend on disease stage, other pathology, and time.
Supporting evidence
Benedet et al. (2020), R05
Tau-PET-related NfL associations were reported in cognitively impaired groups.
This provides conditional support for a relationship, not a universal rule.
DOI: 10.1093/brain/awaa342
Mattsson et al. (2017), R06
NfL was associated with neurodegenerative measures and clinical impairment.
DOI: 10.1001/jamaneurol.2016.6117
Challenging evidence
Salvadó et al. (2023), R17
NfL did not show significant independent associations with plaque or tangle burden in the
investigated postmortem sample.
DOI: 10.15252/emmm.202217123Bianco et al. (2026), R19
NfL was associated with lower cortical thickness rather than the principal quantified amyloid and
tau pathological measures in the overall study.
DOI: 10.1002/alz.71883
Dewenter et al. (2026), R20
NfL was particularly associated with vascular imaging burden.
DOI: 10.1002/alz.71530
Expected relationship
No directional prediction was adopted.
A positive relationship is mechanistically plausible but not guaranteed.
A weak or statistically nonsignificant association would not imply that NfL is biologically
unimportant.
7. Optional Comparative Research
Question — RQ5
Exact previously selected wording
Does the standardized GFAP–amyloid association differ from the standardized
GFAP–tau association?
Status: Optional secondary extension; not a required primary or secondary hypothesis.
Scientific purpose
Determine whether GFAP shows different conditional associations with amyloid and tau PET.
Proposed statistical comparison
Under a defensible common standardization framework:
● Null: β_GFAP,A − β_GFAP,T = 0● Alternative: β_GFAP,A − β_GFAP,T ≠ 0
A direct coefficient-contrast test with the appropriate joint covariance estimate would be
required.
Critical interpretation
One significant coefficient and one nonsignificant coefficient do not establish that the
coefficients differ significantly.
Furthermore, standardized PET coefficients can still be affected by:
● Different measurement reliability.
● Different pathology distributions.
● Different tracer properties.
● Different biological dynamic ranges.
Decision
Do not make RQ5 a mandatory confirmatory analysis without first establishing that the
comparison is scientifically interpretable.

9. Final Measurement Framework
9.1 Amyloid pathology
Selected concept: Continuous cerebral amyloid burden measured with amyloid PET.
Final tracer: Not established.
Final PET scale: Not established.
Final cortical composite: Not established.
Final reference region: Not established.
Final clinical positivity threshold: Not established.
The primary analysis does not require a binary amyloid-positivity classification.
9.2 Tau pathology
Selected concept: Continuous regional tau PET, reflecting tracer-measured aggregated tau
burden.
Final tracer: Not established.
Final primary anatomical region: Not established.
Final PET scale: Not established.
Final reference region: Not established.Final positivity threshold: Not established.
A biologically justified regional measure is preferred over indiscriminately averaging all available
tau PET features.
9.3 Important measurement distinctions
Amyloid PET, tau PET, plasma phosphorylated tau, and CSF tau represent different biological or
imaging measurements.
They are not interchangeable.
Tau PET does not measure plasma p-tau217.
GFAP should not be treated as a direct measurement of amyloid plaques.
NfL should not be treated as a direct measurement of tau tangles.
9.4 PET-defined groups
The original proposal considered four profiles:
Profile Descriptive interpretation
A−T− Neither PET marker meets its specified positivity threshold
A+T− Amyloid-positive, tau-negative PET profile
A−T+ Amyloid-negative, tau-positive PET profile
A+T+ Both PET markers meet their positivity thresholds
Final decision: These groups are not required for the primary model.
Because validated thresholds have not been established, no SPHERE record has been
assigned a finalized clinical group within this phase.
The profiles must not automatically be called different Alzheimer's disease subtypes.
In particular, A−T+ should not be described as a confirmed tau-predominant form of Alzheimer's

11. Final Statistical Analysis Framework
11.1 Study design
Cross-sectional observational association analysis using Stanford SPHERE synthetic data.
11.2 Primary model
Conceptual model:
Standardized GFAP = intercept + βA(standardized amyloid PET) + βT(standardized tau
PET) + βAge(age) + βSex(sex) + error
Primary estimand: βA.
This estimates the conditional association of amyloid burden with GFAP while holding tau PET
and specified covariates constant.
The exact coding and scaling decisions must be documented before fitting the final model.
11.3 Secondary NfL model
Standardized NfL = intercept + βA(standardized amyloid PET) + βT(standardized tau PET)
+ βAge(age) + βSex(sex) + error
H3 uses the NfL model's amyloid coefficient.
H4 uses the NfL model's tau coefficient.H2 uses the tau coefficient of the primary GFAP model.

13. Alternative Hypotheses and Designs
Considered
Alternative A — Original A+T− versus A−T+ study
Original H1: GFAP expected to be higher in A+T−.
Original H2: NfL expected to be higher in A−T+.
Decision: Replaced as the primary design.
Reasons:
● Exact GFAP contrast already investigated.
● NfL directional justification weak.
● Discordant group sample sizes unknown.
● Positivity thresholds not validated.
● A−T+ is not necessarily Alzheimer's disease.
● Binary categorization discards continuous information.
Alternative B — Four AT-group ANCOVA
Compare GFAP and NfL across A−T−, A+T−, A−T+, and A+T+ groups.
Decision: Conditional descriptive or secondary work only.
Reason: Group definitions, clinical meaning, and precision remain unresolved.Alternative C — Continuous GFAP conditional amyloid
model
Test GFAP against amyloid while including tau PET, age, and sex.
Decision: Selected primary design.
Reason: Stronger empirical justification and fewer dependencies on arbitrary PET cutoffs.
Alternative D — Cognitive-stage-specific NfL
associations
Examine whether NfL associations change with cognitive impairment.
Decision: Deferred.
Reasons: Prior literature already addresses stage modification; the available common sample
may be too small for stable interaction tests.
Alternative E — Direct standardized coefficient
comparison
Compare GFAP–amyloid and GFAP–tau coefficients.
Decision: Optional RQ5.

Explore additional molecular predictors or build classification models.
Decision: Outside this project's focused primary protocol.
Reasons: Greater multiple-testing burden, more complicated biological interpretation, and a

Assessment
Original proposed gap
The initial proposal claimed that blood GFAP and NfL had not been directly compared against
amyloid and tau brain pathology in the same participants.
This claim is unsupported and must not appear in the manuscript.
Existing literature includes:
● Direct GFAP/amyloid/tau PET relationships.
● NfL relationships with both PET modalities.
● Simultaneous blood biomarker panels.
● PET-defined AT groups.
● Postmortem plaque and tangle comparisons.
● Biomarker timing and disease-stage analyses.
Pan et al. (2025) specifically addressed the original GFAP discordant-group comparison.
Revised defensible gap
The available literature does not establish how the specified conditional GFAP and NfL
associations will behave in the analyzed Stanford ADRC SPHERE synthetic release using a
harmonized, prespecified, mutually adjusted model.
The study can assess consistency with previously published relationships and sensitivity to
justified PET measurement definitions.
Originality classification
Dataset-specific replication and methodological extension.
Not:
● A newly discovered biomarker relationship.
● The first comparison of GFAP and NfL with amyloid and tau.
● An independently validated clinical discovery.
● Evidence of a novel causal mechanism.
Limitations of this contributionA different dataset or statistical adjustment alone does not guarantee strong biological
originality.
SPHERE is based on the Stanford ADRC cohort. Possible source-cohort overlap with earlier
Stanford work is not resolved.
An independent real-data cohort would provide a stronger replication.
Whether NHSJS considers this contribution sufficiently original remains an editorial question.
15. Synthetic Data and Scientific Validity
The Stanford SPHERE method is designed to preserve important statistical properties while
protecting real participant information.
The methods preprint makes strong claims concerning preservation of linear statistics under its
mathematical framework.
However, the Stanford ADRC implementation includes:
● Missingness restoration.
● Modality-specific availability.
● Synthetic participant-level values.
● Potentially noncontemporaneous source measurements.
The report notes that released per-modality second-order preservation can be close rather than
exact.
Final interpretation rule
Do not assume that every regression performed on a selected, merged, transformed, or
subgroup-restricted SPHERE sample is numerically identical to its real-data counterpart.
The conditions required for fidelity must be examined for the actual analysis.
Additional limitations
● Synthetic rows do not represent individual real patients.
● Inferences cannot automatically be generalized to clinical practice.
● Cross-sectional associations cannot establish temporal ordering.
● Blood and PET timing may differ.
● Measurement uncertainty may influence estimates.● Independent validation has not been completed.
Study attribution
The applicable Stanford data-use agreement and methods documentation must be followed.
The SPHERE methods citation is:
He Z, et al. Unlocking Sensitive Data with SPHERE in the Age of AI. bioRxiv (2026). DOI:
10.64898/2026.09.01.748580.
This was a preprint in the evidence review and is not counted as one of the 20 peer-reviewed
empirical papers.
The Stanford source acknowledgment specified in the project guidance is:
"Stanford Alzheimer's Disease Research Center, NIH/NIA grant P30 AG066515."
The published Stanford DUA provides for a 30-day manuscript review and a 10-day abstract
review before submission or public disclosure, with a possible additional delay of up to 30 days
for specified reasons.
The exact agreement accepted by the data recipient must be checked.
